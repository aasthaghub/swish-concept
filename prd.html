Swish Experience PRD

Author Aastha GulatiStatus Draft for discussionBasis Personal teardown, Play Store reviews, user-journey review, working prototype

Summary

Swish promises food in minutes. The product around that promise has gaps in three places: what happens when things go wrong, how fast a user finds what they came for, and how well the home understands who is opening it and when.

  • Fix recovery first. Support, refunds and delivery confirmation decide whether a bug becomes a 1-star review.
  • Make the home understand context. Time of day, history, diet and festival all change what a user wants. Swish owns its kitchens, so it can tag and shape the menu better than anyone.
  • Cut the time to first add. A fast delivery promise needs a fast order.
The ask: agree the five workstreams, pick the first two experiments, and tell me which baselines I should work from.

Who it is for

One core persona, in four situations that change what the home should show.

Core persona

Bangalore, Gen Z, corporate job, cares about protein, orders most weekday lunches and many late nights. A repeat customer.

Returning user

Has history. Wants their usual in one tap and a checkout with nothing to re-enter.

New user

Came for speed. Has no history, and the full menu is the first obstacle. Their first order decides retention.

Festival user

For example during Navratri: veg by default, some with no onion or garlic. Non-veg in a highlighted row reads as "the app doesn't know what day it is".

Craving-first user

The "11 PM pizza person". Not a health-first user, and should not be lectured.

Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Raise repeat orders per user
  • Cut the time from app open to first add-to-cart
  • Reduce support-driven 1-star reviews
  • Raise first-order conversion for new users

Non-goals

  • No change to the 10-minute delivery promise or kitchen model
  • No new pricing or discount strategy
  • No rebrand. The colour work is a tidy-up of the existing green identity
  • No heavy ML in phase one. Rules first

Principles

  • Recovery has to be as fast as delivery
  • Show, don't make the user hunt
  • Personalise gently, never block the user
  • Every claim on screen must be verifiable

Roadmap

Sequenced by effort and risk. Durations are rough and need engineering input.

Phase 1
Weeks 1–2
Quick wins and the support hub: dish details on every dish (E3), tile contrast (B2), macros on cards (B3), colour clean-up and slim promo strip (E1), support instant-fix buttons (A1).
Phase 2
Weeks 3–6
Recovery and trust: late-order decision (A2), auto-refund (A3), free items as SKUs (A7), customer code and pin confirmation (A4, A5). Plus pinned Protein Picks (B1), Order again (C1) and 1-tap order (D1).
Phase 3
Weeks 7–12
Context layer: Meal Clock (C3), mood pills (C5), balanced plates, crave smart and yogurt bowls (B4–B6), new-user shortcuts (D2), late-night grid (D3), Menu button redesign (E2).
Ongoing
Festival layer (C8) is a calendar-driven switch. Build it once and fill it for upcoming festival windows. Trending (C2), budget zone (C6) and craving-first mode (C7) follow once history and tagging are in place.

Risks and mitigations

RiskMitigation
Instant refunds and 2x credits get abusedTier by value and history, cap repeat claims, use the dispatch scan as evidence, track abuse as a guardrail
Kitchen swap is not always possibleShow the option only when a nearby kitchen has stock and a rider free. Otherwise offer wait or refund
Wrong persona inference annoys usersKeep the pills and menu always available, let users correct it, never label them out loud
Personalisation lowers discovery or basket sizeCap the usuals row at three items, keep first-time dish share and AOV as guardrails
Nutrition and "vrat" claims are wrongPublish only what kitchens can verify, label approximate values, and check claims against food-labelling rules before launch
Customer code slows handoverOffer leave-at-door with photo and hand-to-guard, remember the choice, measure door time
Casual copy clashes with brand voiceAgree a tone guide first, A/B against plain labels

Open questions for Swish

Data and constraints that would change the plan.

  1. What are the current day-7 and day-30 retention and repeat rates, split by new and returning users?
  2. What share of orders are late or cancelled, and what is the refund rate and cost?
  3. How many support contacts per 100 orders, and which five reasons are the biggest?
  4. How complete is dish-level tagging: calories, protein, veg, onion and garlic, comfort, junk?
  5. Can the rider app move delivery completion to a customer code, and what are the gateway's refund options?
  6. How much spare kitchen and rider capacity is there for a "swap kitchen" option?
  7. How does Swish GO relate to the main app, and should these changes cover both?
